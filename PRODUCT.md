# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Technical recruiters, hiring managers, and senior engineers evaluating a candidate for a Junior / New-Grad Full-Stack Developer role. They arrive with a specific job in mind, scan quickly for signal, and want evidence of architectural thinking — not just visual craft. They are comfortable reading dense technical information and are skeptical of portfolios that prioritize decoration over substance.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Omar Mohamed, a senior CS student positioning himself as a highly capable Junior Full-Stack Developer. The site exists to convert a first impression into a hiring conversation. Success looks like a recruiter or senior engineer saying "this person understands how to build complete systems" and reaching out. Secondary goal: demonstrate that the same engineering discipline that built the projects also built the portfolio itself.

## Brand Personality

Engineered, precise, structured. The portfolio should feel like a well-calibrated tool or technical dashboard — not a magazine, not an agency showcase. Voice is direct and confident without being boastful. Copy reads like a senior engineer describing their work to a peer: specific, evidence-based, no filler adjectives.

## Anti-references

- **Creative agency bounce sites**: excessive scroll-jacking, oversized cartoony illustrations, floating emojis, noisy or chaotic layouts. These read as "front-end decorator," not "systems builder."
- **Over-designed portfolio templates**: glassmorphism as a default surface, gradient text everywhere, hero-metric layouts with big numbers and sparklines, identical card grids.
- **Playful-first portfolios**: fun is not the brief. Approachability comes from competence and clarity, not from whimsy.

## Design Principles

1. **Functional over decorative.** Every design choice must justify itself as information or structure. If a visual element isn't conveying hierarchy, state, or relationship, it shouldn't exist.
2. **Precision signals competence.** Tight spacing, exact alignment, 1px border geometry, and consistent spacing scales are not aesthetic choices — they are evidence of the engineer who built the site. Sloppiness in the UI undermines the claim of engineering discipline.
3. **Cyan is a signal, not a paint.** The brand accent is reserved for interactive state, focus rings, active nav indicators, data highlights, and call-to-action anchors. It is never used as a background fill, gradient source, or decoration.
4. **Density with deliberate rhythm.** Information-dense like a technical surface, but spacing varies intentionally to create grouping and hierarchy. No flat uniform padding; generous separation between sections, tight grouping within them.
5. **Motion is functional.** Transitions reveal structure or confirm state — they don't perform. No bounce, no scroll-jacking, no entrance animations for their own sake. Reduced-motion users get the same information with instant transitions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as the minimum bar. Body text ≥ 4.5:1 contrast against its background; large text ≥ 3:1. All interactive elements keyboard-navigable with visible focus states. Reduced-motion respected via `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)`. No content gated behind animation completion.
